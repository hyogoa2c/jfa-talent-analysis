# Institution × Era → Head-Coach Timeline Pilot (2026-07-10)

Executes the pilot phase of `docs/coach_network_design_2026-07-10.md`: can 2000-2025
head-coach timelines be reconstructed for the head youth-development institutions? 15
institutions tested (5 universities / 5 high schools / 5 J-club academies, drawn from the
top of the player-stint concentration distribution).

Research performed by a Sonnet subagent (institution articles → coach-biography chaining →
official sites/JFA archives), written incrementally; the subagent hit a session limit after
completing all 15 institutions and both summary sections, losing only its final status
message. The coordinator independently re-verified two claims before accepting: 市立船橋's
full four-coach chain (布啓一郎→石渡靖之→朝岡隆蔵→波多秀吾, confirmed via 布's own
retrospective interview and current JFA team pages) and 前橋育英's 山田耕介 1982-present
tenure (Wikipedia + multiple profiles). Both matched exactly.

### Source types that worked, by institution type

- **High schools:** Overwhelmingly the strongest category, against the pilot's expectation. Every one of the 5 pilot high schools is nationally prominent (multiple 全国高校選手権 titles), which means (a) the school/club has extensive dedicated press coverage (Number Web, サカイク, スポーツナビ, local newspapers) and (b) each individual coach — being a minor celebrity in the high-school-football world — has either a Wikipedia article or multiple long-form profile pieces that state exact tenure years, predecessors, and successors. Chaining through *coaches'* biographies rather than the *institution's* Wikipedia article was consistently the highest-yield method.
- **Universities:** Mixed. The 3 universities with a single long-tenured "franchise" coach (流通経済大学, and to a lesser extent 明治大学) were as easy as the high schools. Universities with higher coach turnover, an institutional Wikipedia article that just isn't very developed, or (in 国士舘大学's case) a scandal that displaced normal press coverage of staffing, left multi-year gaps. The official club site's own **per-season staff pages** (discovered at 法政大学, `hoseifc.com/club/season/YYYY/`) turned out to be an under-used primary source not anticipated in the original method — worth checking for every university before falling back to search.
- **J-club academies (hardest, as expected):** Only FC東京 had a purpose-built Wikipedia article with a complete 歴代監督 table — this is likely because FC東京's youth setup is unusually well documented, not representative of most clubs. For the other four academies, Wikipedia coverage of the youth team is thin-to-nonexistent, and general web search surfaces only recent (last 3–5 years) transitions reliably. The two methods that DID work for J-academies: (1) official club news releases for appointment/retirement announcements (exact dates, but only found for transitions the search engine already knew about — a chicken-and-egg problem for older transitions), and (2) **archived JFA Premier League / Jリーグ.jp Jユースカップ year-specific team-introduction pages**, which name that season's 監督 directly and were the single most valuable discovery of this pilot for the J-academy category. Their major limitation: team ID/slot numbers in the URL pattern are **not stable across years** (the same numeric slot points to a different club in different seasons), so each year has to be located individually — a real, non-trivial scaling cost.
- **The 総監督/監督 (and analogous アカデミーダイレクター/現場監督) duality** was confirmed as a real, recurring structural pattern — found explicitly at 法政大学 (照井博康 総監督 + rotating 監督) and セレッソ大阪U-18 (director-level "monitor" title vs. working on-field coach), and suspected but unconfirmed at 流通経済大学 and 青森山田. Any scaled build needs a schema with (at minimum) a `role_type` field distinguishing 総監督/ディレクター from on-field 監督, since collapsing them into one "coach" column would silently merge two different people's tenures or misattribute authority.
- **Year-boundary imprecision (±1 year)** was common even in otherwise "solid" cases (明治大学, 法政大学, 東福岡高等学校) — very plausibly caused by Japanese sports' April-start season vs. calendar-year press reporting conventions. A scaled build should probably store tenure as "season starting April of year X" rather than a bare year, and treat ±1-year discrepancies between sources as expected noise rather than a data-quality flag.

### Best and worst cases

- **Best case: 市立船橋高等学校サッカー部.** An unbroken, exactly month-dated chain of 4 coaches spanning 1983–present (布啓一郎 → 石渡靖之 → 朝岡隆蔵 → 波多秀吾), with every transition independently corroborated by two or more sources explicitly naming the predecessor/successor pair.
- **Worst case: 柏レイソルU-18.** Only ~25% of the 2000–2025 window could be reconstructed. The academy's Wikipedia article is narrative/philosophical rather than a coach roster, general search surfaced almost nothing before 2020, and even the specialized football-database site that looked most promising by title (Soccer D.B.'s "監督比較" page) returned a blocked/404 response when fetched.

## Recommendation

**Scaling to the full ~100 institutions is feasible, but expected data completeness and per-institution effort differ sharply by type, and the method should be prioritized accordingly.** High schools should be prioritized first and can likely be processed with a lightweight, mostly-automated version of this method (institution search → chain through named coaches' own bios) — expect ~30–45 minutes of research effort per school and ~90%+ coverage for any nationally-prominent program, though lower-profile regional schools not in this pilot may perform worse than 東福岡/青森山田-caliber programs did. Universities should be treated as medium-priority and medium-effort (~45–75 minutes each): check the institution's own official per-season staff pages FIRST (a pattern discovered mid-pilot, not in the original method) before falling back to Wikipedia/search, and expect roughly 1-in-5 to have an unrecoverable multi-year gap similar to 国士舘大学. **J-club academies should be deprioritized or budgeted separately with 2–3x the effort per institution** (~90–120 minutes each): only chase the flagship, extensively-covered academies (FC東京-caliber) with the standard method, and for the rest, budget time specifically to hunt year-by-year through archived JFA/Jリーグ.jp team-introduction pages (noting the URL-slot-instability problem) — or consider that some multi-year gaps in this category may simply be unrecoverable from public web sources at any reasonable effort level, and the project should decide in advance how much missing-data tolerance the eventual coach-effects analysis can absorb for this institution type.
